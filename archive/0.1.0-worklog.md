# 0.1.0 - worklog

What was built, in the order it happened, and how each step was validated.

## 1. field and noise (`src/rng.js`, `src/noise.js`, `src/field.js`)

seeded mulberry32 + 2d simplex with a shuffled permutation table, fbm on top.
the field module owns: the height field, its gradient, the curl of a second noise
field, the guide curve (catmull rom -> polyline -> distance grid with the closest
point's arc length and tangent), the gate wells (entry hill, exit pit) and the beacon
cones.

validation: `experiments/forces.js` prints the mean and max of the field derivatives
and the per layer force breakdown along the corridor. this is the tool that made the
balance measurable instead of guessed.

pitfall hit: the arc length field is discontinuous across the medial axis of the
curve, its gradient exploded and trapped every body in the middle of the corridor.
fixed by pushing along the polyline tangent instead.

## 2. transport models (`src/flow.js`)

gas pressure: a source at the entry and a sink at the exit generate a pressure
potential (poisson solve, gauss seidel, 2500 sweeps), the flow is `-grad phi`,
normalised so the 90th percentile of the speed is the target. the medium drags the
ship with it; the gas cloud is drawn from the tracer particles.

pitfall hit: the potential flow ran backwards (a source has a *high* potential, so the
velocity is `-grad phi`), and the source field was normalised by the net sum, which
nearly cancels between source and sink and flips the field.

## 3. world and mechanics (`src/sim.js`)

ship, gates, tracers, resources, metrics. environment composition:

```
noise gradient  -grad h            (heightmap drift)
curl            curl psi           (divergence free swirl)
valley          -k * d * grad d    (the further from the guide, the stronger)
channel floor   +ramp * tangent    (the channel slopes down to the exit, chan weighted)
beacons         cone forces        (entry pushes, exit tractors)
gate wells      -grad phi_gate     (analytic mode only, the gas replaces them)
```

the channel is calm inside (turbulence x0.8) and brutal outside (x3.2). the guide
curve is therefore not decoration: it is the only thing that creates a route.

mechanics from the draft: gates are built (350 cr), opened (45 cr + 30 energy) and
kept open (3 cr/s + 0.8 energy/s); the engines burn energy; rough fields wear the
hull and the mind; a fast or crooked entry is deflected and damages the ship; the
jump itself costs 35 energy.

validation: `experiments/probe.js` (one flight, printed every 2 s),
`experiments/trace.js` (one flight, printed every second, shows the distance to the
exit and the offset from the guide).

## 4. reference pilot and trials (`src/autopilot.js`, `src/stats.js`)

the pilot aims at a point on the guide curve ahead of the ship, slows down on
approach, and its aim degrades with the ship's mental strain. it is a *reference*
pilot: it makes configurations comparable, it is not a player model.

`runTrial` flies one seed headless, `probePath` runs the medium alone and reports the
share of released tracers that reach the sink (the "does a natural path exist"
metric, independent of the ship).

pitfall hit: the first version measured the natural path on the tracers of a flight,
so the number depended on when the flight ended. it is a separate probe now.

## 5. page (`index.html`, `style.css`, `src/render.js`, `src/ui.js`, `src/main.js`)

file:// friendly, classic script tags, no build. canvas with the potential heatmap or
the gas cloud, the guide channel, the beacon cones, the tracers, the ship with a
trail and an engine flame, a strain vignette that grows with the mental state.
panel: mode checkboxes, eight sliders, gate cards, a trial runner with a result
table, hull/energy/mind gauges and a readout.

validation: `experiments/smoke.js` loads the page in a headless dom with a stubbed 2d
context, flips a mode, moves a slider, runs the trials and fails on any console
error. `experiments/shot.js` renders a frame to a png (`@napi-rs/canvas`) so the
visuals can be inspected without a browser.

## 6. measurements (`experiments/sweep.js`)

24 flights per configuration, seed base 4242, plus a difficulty curve over the
turbulence strength. results are appended to `experiments/logs/`.

| configuration | arrived | avg time | hull left | natural path |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| analytic + guide | 79% | 9.6 s | 81 | 88% |
| analytic, no guide | 46% | 16.8 s | 84 | 5% |
| analytic, no beacons | 100% | 11.3 s | 98 | 83% |
| analytic, no noise | 96% | 9.9 s | 83 | 88% |
| analytic, no curl | 79% | 9.6 s | 85 | 88% |
| gas + guide | 75% | 8.5 s | 83 | 75% |
| gas, no guide | 79% | 11.5 s | 85 | 72% |

what it says:

- **the guide curve is the difference between a route and a guess.** without it only
  5% of the released tracers reach the sink: the heightmap has traps and bodies stop
  in them. a steered ship still improvises 46% of the time, but it takes 1.75x longer
  and it is luck.
- **the gas pressure transport is robust by construction.** 72% of the gas reaches the
  sink even with no guide curve, because the pressure field always routes to the
  drain. this is the alternative transport model of the draft working as intended.
- **the beacons are a tax, not a help.** removing them takes the arrival rate from
  79% to 100% and the hull loss from 19% to 2%: the entry launch wave and the exit
  tractor fight a ship that is already fighting the medium.
- **the curl is the expensive layer.** the noise drift is nearly free (96% without
  it), the curl costs 21 points of the success rate: it pushes perpendicular to the
  heightmap gradient, so it cannot be countered by aiming down the slope.
- **difficulty is a curve, not a switch.** the reference pilot holds the trip together
  up to about 2x turbulence and then it falls apart:

| turbulence | analytic + guide | gas + guide | natural path |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.5 | 96% | 83% | 88% |
| 1 | 79% | 75% | 88% |
| 1.5 | 75% | 71% | 87% |
| 2 | 58% | 71% | 81% |
| 2.5 | 67% | 50% | 74% |
| 3 | 46% | 63% | 64% |
| 4 | 29% | 50% | 46% |

  the gas transport keeps roughly twice as many flights alive at 4x turbulence
  (50% against 29%) - the pressure route survives a medium that the heightmap
  route does not.
