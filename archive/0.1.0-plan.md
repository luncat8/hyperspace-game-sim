# 0.1.0 - hyperspace corridor: path-finding difficulty

Goal of the project (from the README draft): investigate how hard it is to find your
way through hyperspace. Far travel goes through a short path that links two scales of
reality: fly `x` km through hyperspace, come out `x*k` km away in another universe.

The sim stays oriented on *game mechanics* first: it is a playable prototype plus a
measurement rig, not a pretty noise demo.

## 1. what is simulated

A 2D "hyperspace corridor" between two gates, 2200 x 1300 world units:

* `ENTRY` gate - source. It launches the ship (a beacon cone that pushes along the
  gate axis), spawns tracer particles / gas and is a *hill* in the potential.
* `EXIT` gate - sink. It pulls with a tractor beacon cone, is a *pit* in the
  potential and captures the ship when the ship enters the mouth aligned and slow
  enough. A fast or crooked entry is deflected and damages the hull.

The ship is a drifting body: it has thrust and drag, but the medium pushes it
around. Getting from the entry to the exit is the path-finding problem.

Environment layers, each a toggle so variants can be compared:

| layer | mechanic |
| --- | --- |
| noise drift | the gradient of a simplex fbm pushes the ship downhill (heightmap) |
| curl turbulence | the curl of a second noise field: divergence free, pushes perpendicular to the heightmap gradient, cannot be countered by aiming down the slope |
| guide channel | a curve between the gates. distance to the curve is a valley (the force grows with the distance), the channel floor slopes down towards the exit, and the medium inside the channel is calm while outside it is 4x rougher |
| gate beacons | narrow light cones from the gates push/pull the ship |
| gas pressure | alternative transport: a pressure field routes everything to the sink, so a route exists no matter what the heightmap does |

Costs and dangers from the draft: gates are built (350 cr), opened (45 cr + 30
energy) and kept open (3 cr/s + 0.8 energy/s); the engines burn energy; the jump
costs 35 energy; rough fields wear the hull and the mind; a deflected gate costs
hull. Resources are simulated but the trials run with both gates open, so the
measurement is about navigation and not about the economy.

## 2. transport models

*analytic* (default): the ship is pushed by the noise gradient + curl + gate wells.
There is no guaranteed route - the heightmap has traps and bodies stop in them.

*gas pressure*: a source at the entry and a sink at the exit generate a pressure
potential (poisson solve on a grid). The flow always routes to the sink, so a
natural path exists whatever the heightmap does. The environment still turbulences
the gas, so the ship still has to be steered.

The difference between the two models is the question of the project: does a
guaranteed medium level route make the trip easy, and what does the player still
have to do?

## 3. architecture

file:// friendly, classic script tags, no build, node runnable modules.

```
src/rng.js       seeded PRNG
src/noise.js     seeded 2D simplex noise + fbm
src/field.js     analytic field: noise, curl, guide distance field, gate wells, beacons
src/flow.js      gas pressure model: poisson potential -> flow, gas cloud from particles
src/sim.js       world state: ship, gates, tracers, mechanics, metrics
src/autopilot.js reference pilot used for repeatable trials
src/stats.js     trial runner, natural path probe, aggregation
src/render.js    canvas rendering
src/ui.js        control panel, hud, keyboard
src/main.js      bootstrap + fixed step loop
```

Every file is an IIFE attached to `globalThis.HSG` and exports through
`module.exports` under node, so the same code powers the page and the experiments.
The modules must not depend on the DOM: `sim`, `field`, `flow`, `autopilot` and
`stats` run headless.

The environment composition for any body (ship, tracer):

```
noise gradient  -grad h * 11000 * turbulence * channelTurb
curl            curl psi * 6500 * turbulence * channelTurb
valley          -1.6 * d * grad d            (d = distance to the guide curve)
channel floor   +240 * rampStrength * tangent * channelFactor
beacons         cone force (entry pushes, exit tractors)
gate wells      -grad phi_gate               (analytic mode only)
```

with `channelTurb = 0.8` inside the channel and `3.2` outside it, and
`channelFactor` falling to zero at 2.6 channel widths.

Ship: thrust 85 u/s^2, drag 0.62 /s, max speed 540, reactor 2.5 energy/s, engine
cost 0.6 energy/s at full thrust, hull 100, mind 100. Hull wear and mind loss scale
with the turbulence intensity, the mind degrades the pilot's aim, the hull loss
scales with the turbulence intensity too.

Gate capture: within radius + 26, in front of the mouth, within 0.62 rad of the
axis, slower than 280 u/s. Otherwise the gate deflects the ship.

## 4. metrics (the actual investigation)

* natural path: the share of tracer particles released at the entry that reach the
  exit, measured on the medium alone (`probePath`) - does a route exist?
* ship trials (headless, reference pilot, many seeds): success rate, time to
  arrival, hull left, energy used, path length vs straight line, worst deviation
  from the guide curve, re-entries into the entry gate.

`experiments/sweep.js` runs the configurations and appends to `experiments/logs/`.

## 5. ui

canvas plus a side panel: mode checkboxes, eight environment sliders, gate cards
with their costs, a trial runner with a result table, gauges for hull / energy /
mind and a readout with the time, speed, distance to the exit and the offset from
the guide curve. Keyboard: `W`/`S` engines, `A`/`D` turn, `space` brake,
`shift` full burn.

## 6. results of the reference runs (24 flights per configuration)

| configuration | arrived | avg time | hull left | natural path |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| analytic + guide | 79% | 9.6 s | 81 | 88% |
| analytic, no guide | 46% | 16.8 s | 84 | 5% |
| analytic, no beacons | 100% | 11.3 s | 98 | 83% |
| analytic, no noise | 96% | 9.9 s | 83 | 88% |
| analytic, no curl | 79% | 9.6 s | 85 | 88% |
| gas + guide | 75% | 8.5 s | 83 | 75% |
| gas, no guide | 79% | 11.5 s | 85 | 72% |

- the guide curve is the difference between a route and a guess: without it 5% of
  the released tracers reach the sink, the rest stop in the traps of the heightmap
- the gas pressure transport is robust by construction: 72% of the gas reaches the
  sink even with no guide curve
- the beacons are a tax: without them the arrival rate goes to 100%
- the curl is the expensive layer, the noise drift is nearly free
- difficulty is a curve: the trip survives turbulence up to about 2.5x
